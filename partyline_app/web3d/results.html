Plain-English reading

What this does not show

  • Aircraft paths are recorded ADS-B; clearances and the radio channel are simulated.
  • Real traffic is already separated by controllers, so conflicts are near-passes within the chosen thresholds.
  • Results come from the recorded airspaces and time periods only. More airspaces and times of day are needed.
  • Intervals assume the time windows are independent; neighbouring windows can share traffic patterns.
  • Many comparisons were made on the same data, so isolated significant results should be read with care.
  • The coding gain over an equally-improved IDNC is small. Most of the improvement comes from the scheduling policy (hard-deadline rule, tiered repair batching), which any coder can use.
  • Tiered repair saves transmissions by letting repairs wait a bounded time: aircraft that missed a clearance get it a few seconds later, and slightly more old clearances are withdrawn before delivery (strict metric).
  • The ground station is assumed to know every aircraft's reception state (free feedback), for all schemes alike.
  • Awareness demand is a model, not a measurement: every other aircraft in the same sector (or within a radius) should receive a clearance within a soft deadline. The deadline and sector size are assumptions; the study repeats the result with other values.
  • Feedback is not free in the channel-time columns: broadcast schemes need state reports from many aircraft, so they use more channel time than addressed delivery. Addressed delivery is cheap only because it misses most deadlines at this load.
  • No VDL Mode 2 collision or access-delay model beyond a fixed background load; the channel is a simplified loss model.
