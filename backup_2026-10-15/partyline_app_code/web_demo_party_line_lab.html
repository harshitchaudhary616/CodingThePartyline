Party Line Lab

Does index coding help CPDLC clearances reach the aircraft that need them, before the deadline? Simulated traffic, real code, all results reproducible.
Read this first. This is a simulation on synthetic traffic. In our runs, coded transmission saves only 0.3% to 3.4% of transmissions compared with plain broadcast, and the gain grows with traffic and packet loss. The big saving is against sending every clearance as a separate addressed message, which is what today's datalink does.

Caveats on every number above

  • All traffic is synthetic. Clearances are generated by a simple controller model, not by real controllers.
  • About a third of all pairs miss their deadline under every scheme, mostly because the predicted conflict is already inside the response margin when the clearance is issued. That must be separated out before any miss rate is quoted in a paper.
  • The channel is a queueing model with random access delay and loss, not a full VDL Mode 2 simulation.
  • Side information is limited to each aircraft's own clearance plus what it has decoded. Richer side information is the main open experiment.

How one transmission can serve two aircraft

        

          

          

          
        

        
        

      

      

        
The model in one page

        
    
          
  • Each aircraft holds its own clearance (sent by the usual addressed message) plus every clearance it has decoded since.
    • 
          
  • A clearance is relevant to another aircraft when their predicted paths pass within the relevance radius inside the look-ahead horizon, or they share a resource such as the runway sequence.
    • 
          
  • Each aircraft–clearance pair has a deadline: predicted loss of separation minus processing, crew response and manoeuvre time.
    • 
          
  • A transmission is the XOR of a set of equal-length messages. An aircraft decodes message m only if it already holds every other message in the set.
    • 
          
  • Schemes see identical scenarios, channel and information. Only transmission choice differs.
    • 
          
  • ADS-B is used to decide relevance only. It is never treated as decoding side information.
    • 
        

        
What the result means

        
Plain broadcast already restores the shared "party line" because every aircraft hears every clearance. Coding can only beat it when several clearances wait at once and the aircraft that need them hold exactly the right other ones. In these runs that almost never happens.

      

      

        
Decision for the paper

        
    
          
  • not supported "Coding substantially reduces datalink load versus broadcast."
    • 
          
  • supported "Addressed messaging cannot sustain party-line coverage; broadcast restores it at a fraction of the load."
    • 
          
  • lead The gain grows with load and loss (3.4% fewer transmissions and 3.5 points fewer misses at 3 arrivals/min, 30% loss). Whether richer side information widens it is the main open experiment.
